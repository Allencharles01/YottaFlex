**Key design points**
- **Targets:** NORMAL and COMPLIANT, disjoint, ranked by a seeded key. The most constrained type is picked first (pairs → custody → mismatch → logger → missing).
- **Rates:** each rate is a share of *shipments*, so the pair type gets ⌈rate·N/2⌉-style pair counts, with at least 1 per type.
- **product_threshold_mismatch:** a block of readings alone can't pass under the declared class, because the rest of the trip would be out of range there. So the whole trip is held in the overlap of the declared policy's safe band and the product policy's excursion band. Example: a REFRIG product declared COOL_8_15, held at about 8.6–11.9 °C. A checker that trusts the declaration sees a fully compliant stream; the correct policy gives SINGLE plus CUMULATIVE breaches.
- **Pairs:** consecutive assignments of one device. A ends with k_A readings above its range and B starts with k_B. Each is within its allowance, but k_A + k_B exceeds the allowance, so merging by device would invent a breach.
- **Carrier claim:** never changed by injection. It stays PASS, as the Phase 1 catalogue designed.
